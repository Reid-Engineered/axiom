# Synthesis Report: Volumes of Revolution (Cylindrical Shells)

## Source scope
- **Primary Text**: OpenStax *Calculus Volume 2* (Senior Contributing Authors: Gilbert Strang & Edwin "Jed" Herman; OpenStax / Rice University, 2016; Digital ISBN-13: 978-1-947172-14-2).
- **Chapter Analyzed**: Chapter 2 (*Applications of Integration*).
- **Core Section**: Section 2.3 (*Volumes of Revolution: Cylindrical Shells*, pp. 137–152).
- **Supporting Section**: Section 2.2 (*Determining Volumes by Slicing*, pp. 118–137) for comparative methodology and cross-sectional slicing references.

---

## Concepts selected

1. **`shell-method-vertical-axis` (The Method of Cylindrical Shells: Vertical Axis of Revolution)**
   - *Rationale*: This is the central conceptual foundation of Section 2.3 (Rule 2.6). It establishes the geometric decomposition of a 3D solid of revolution into nested cylindrical shells with representative radius $r(x)$ and height $h(x)$, integrated with respect to $x$. It covers standard rotation about the $y$-axis, regions bounded between two curves, and shifted rotation about vertical lines $x = k$.

2. **`shell-method-horizontal-axis` (The Method of Cylindrical Shells: Horizontal Axis of Revolution)**
   - *Rationale*: OpenStax Section 2.3 explicitly formulates the horizontal counterpart rule (p. 142) where rotation occurs about the $x$-axis and integration is performed with respect to $y$, $V = \int_c^d 2\pi y g(y)\,dy$. Isolating this into its own concept allows Axiom Practice and diagnostic tutors to identify variable-of-integration confusion.

3. **`method-selection-volume-of-revolution` (Method Selection for Solids of Revolution)**
   - *Rationale*: OpenStax Section 2.3 devotes a dedicated subsection (*"Which Method Should We Use?"*, pp. 145–147) and Example 2.17 to comparing the cylindrical shells method against the disk and washer methods from Section 2.2. Distinguishing when one method requires fewer integrals or simpler algebraic expressions is an essential higher-order calculus skill.

## Objectives

1. **`shell.setup-radius-height-y-axis`**
   - *Learner ability*: Identify and express the geometric shell radius $r(x)$ and shell height $h(x)$ for a region revolved around a vertical axis (either the $y$-axis or a shifted vertical line $x = k$).
   - *OpenStax basis*: Supported by Section 2.3 derivation (pp. 138–140), Rule 2.6, and Example 2.15 (pp. 142–144).

2. **`shell.compute-volume-y-axis-single-curve`**
   - *Learner ability*: Calculate the exact volume of a solid generated by revolving a region under a single continuous nonnegative function $y = f(x)$ over $[a, b]$ around the $y$-axis using $V = \int_a^b 2\pi x f(x)\,dx$.
   - *OpenStax basis*: Directly supported by Rule 2.6 (p. 140), Example 2.12 ($y = 1/x$), and Example 2.13 ($y = 2x - x^2$).

3. **`shell.compute-volume-y-axis-between-curves`**
   - *Learner ability*: Calculate the exact volume of revolution around the $y$-axis for a region enclosed between two curves $y = f(x)$ and $y = g(x)$ by setting up $h(x) = f(x) - g(x)$.
   - *OpenStax basis*: Directly supported by Example 2.16 (pp. 144–145, region between $y = \sqrt{x}$ and $y = 1/x$) and Checkpoint 2.16 ($y = x$ and $y = x^2$).

4. **`shell.compute-volume-shifted-vertical-axis`**
   - *Learner ability*: Formulate the shifted shell radius $r(x) = |x - k|$ and evaluate the volume of revolution for a region rotated around a non-coordinate vertical line $x = k$.
   - *OpenStax basis*: Directly supported by Example 2.15 (pp. 142–144, revolving $y = x$ around $x = -1$) and Exercises 144–148.

5. **`shell.compute-volume-x-axis`**
   - *Learner ability*: Set up and evaluate the cylindrical shell integral with respect to $y$, $V = \int_c^d 2\pi y g(y)\,dy$, for a region revolved around the $x$-axis.
   - *OpenStax basis*: Directly supported by the Rule for Solids Revolved around the $x$-axis (p. 142) and Example 2.14 ($x = 2\sqrt{y}$ over $[0, 4]$).

6. **`shell.select-optimal-method`**
   - *Learner ability*: Analyze a bounded planar region and choose between slicing (disks/washers) and cylindrical shells based on cross-section alignment and the required number of non-overlapping integrals.
   - *OpenStax basis*: Directly supported by Section 2.3 subsection "Which Method Should We Use?", Figure 2.34, and Example 2.17 (pp. 145–147).

## Problem families

1. **`pf-shell-y-poly` (Polynomial Bounded Region around $y$-axis)**
   - *Educational purpose*: Practice setting up and integrating polynomial shells $V = \int_0^b 2\pi x (c x - x^2)\,dx$.
   - *OpenStax basis*: Example 2.13 ($y = 2x - x^2$) and Checkpoint 2.13 ($y = 3x - x^2$).
   - *Parameterization*: Linear coefficient $c \in [2, 6]$, followed by $b \in [1, c]$ using a machine-readable maximum bound that references `coeff`.
   - *Constraint strategy*: Sampling `coeff` before evaluating `b.max = { parameter: "coeff", offset: 0 }` mechanically enforces $b \le c$, so $f(x) \ge 0$ throughout the integration interval.
   - *Canonical solution*: $V = 2\pi [c b^3/3 - b^4/4]$.
   - *Verification strategy*: Evaluated via `math.verify@1` in `symbolic-equivalence` mode.
   - *Expected difficulty*: 1–2 (Introductory to Standard).
   - *Limitations*: Restricted to quadratic polynomials with root at origin.

2. **`pf-shell-y-reciprocal` (Reciprocal Function Bounded Region)**
   - *Educational purpose*: Reinforce how cylindrical shells simplify functions of the form $k/x$ because $x \cdot (k/x) = k$, turning the integrand into a constant.
   - *OpenStax basis*: Example 2.12 ($y = 1/x$ on $[1, 3]$) and Exercise 128.
   - *Parameterization*: Constant $k \in [1, 8]$, $a \in [1, 4]$, followed by $b \in [a+1, 10]$ using a machine-readable minimum bound that references `a`.
   - *Constraint strategy*: $a \ge 1$ avoids the singularity at $x = 0$; evaluating `b.min = { parameter: "a", offset: 1 }` mechanically guarantees a nonempty interval with $b>a$.
   - *Canonical solution*: $V = 2\pi k (b - a)$.
   - *Verification strategy*: `math.verify@1` symbolic equivalence.
   - *Expected difficulty*: 1 (Foundational).
   - *Limitations*: Only applies to simple hyperbola/reciprocal curves.

3. **`pf-shell-y-between-curves` (Region Between Two Intersecting Curves)**
   - *Educational purpose*: Practice computing the shell height as $h(x) = f(x) - g(x)$ and finding intersection bounds.
   - *OpenStax basis*: Example 2.16 and Checkpoint 2.16 ($y = x$ and $y = x^2$).
   - *Parameterization*: Slope $m \in [1, 4]$ for upper curve $y = mx$ and lower parabola $y = x^2$.
   - *Constraint strategy*: Intersection points are analytically fixed at $x = 0$ and $x = m$. For $x \in [0, m]$, $mx \ge x^2$.
   - *Canonical solution*: $V = 2\pi \int_0^m (mx^2 - x^3)\,dx = \pi m^4 / 6$.
   - *Verification strategy*: `math.verify@1` symbolic equivalence.
   - *Expected difficulty*: 2–3 (Intermediate).
   - *Limitations*: Uses standard polynomial intersections with exact roots.

4. **`pf-shell-shifted-vertical-axis` (Rotation Around Shifted Vertical Axis $x = -k$)**
   - *Educational purpose*: Evaluate learner mastery of adjusted shell radius $r(x) = x - (-k) = x + k$.
   - *OpenStax basis*: Example 2.15 (rotation of $y = x$ on $[1, 2]$ around $x = -1$) and Exercise 144.
   - *Parameterization*: Shift $k \in [1, 3]$, $a \in [1, 2]$, followed by $b \in [a+1, 4]$ using a machine-readable minimum bound that references `a`.
   - *Constraint strategy*: Evaluating `b.min = { parameter: "a", offset: 1 }` prevents degenerate intervals. The axis $x = -k$ remains strictly left of $[a,b]$, so $r(x) = x + k > 0$.
   - *Canonical solution*: $V = 2\pi [(b^3/3 + k b^2/2) - (a^3/3 + k a^2/2)]$.
   - *Verification strategy*: `math.verify@1` symbolic equivalence.
   - *Expected difficulty*: 2–3 (Intermediate).
   - *Limitations*: Covers left-shifted vertical lines; right-shifted lines ($x = +K$ with $K \ge b$) can be added in subsequent packages.

5. **`pf-shell-x-axis-dy` (Shells Revolved Around $x$-axis)**
   - *Educational purpose*: Train the learner to integrate with respect to $y$ when revolving horizontal shells around the $x$-axis.
   - *OpenStax basis*: Rule p. 142, Example 2.14 ($x = 2\sqrt{y}$ on $[0, 4]$), and Exercises 134–137.
   - *Parameterization*: Curve $x = c_{\text{coeff}} \sqrt{y}$, $y \in [0, d]$ with $c_{\text{coeff}} \in [1, 4]$ and $d \in [1, 4]$.
   - *Constraint strategy*: $y \ge 0$ maintains real values for the square root; radius is $r(y) = y$ and height is $x = g(y)$.
   - *Canonical solution*: $V = \int_0^d 2\pi y (c_{\text{coeff}} \sqrt{y})\,dy = (4\pi c_{\text{coeff}} / 5) d^{5/2}$.
   - *Verification strategy*: `math.verify@1` symbolic equivalence.
   - *Expected difficulty*: 2–3 (Intermediate).
   - *Limitations*: Uses power functions with fractional exponents.

6. **`pf-shell-setup-integrand` (Diagnostic Setup Formulation)**
   - *Educational purpose*: Isolate and diagnose the subskill of formulating the shell integrand $2\pi r(x) h(x)$ without the confounding arithmetic of antidifferentiation.
   - *OpenStax basis*: Conceptual derivation steps in Section 2.3 (p. 139, p. 143).
   - *Parameterization*: Exponent $n \in [2, 5]$, axis offset $k \in [1, 5]$.
   - *Constraint strategy*: Generates clean monomials with integer exponents and shifts.
   - *Canonical solution*: $2\pi (x^{n+1} + k x^n)$.
   - *Template convention*: Rendering performs one exact key substitution without arithmetic evaluation. LaTeX grouping braces wrap placeholders, so `x^{{n}+1}` with `n = 2` renders as `x^{2+1}`.
   - *Verification strategy*: `math.verify@1` symbolic equivalence.
   - *Expected difficulty*: 1–2 (Foundational Diagnostic).
   - *Limitations*: Pure setup probe; does not test definite integration arithmetic.

7. **`pf-method-select-integral-count` (Method Selection Diagnostic)**
   - *Educational purpose*: Assess the learner's ability to count required integrals when comparing disk and shell methods on triangular / composite boundaries.
   - *OpenStax basis*: Example 2.17(a) (region bounded by $y = x$, $y = 2 - x$, $y = 0$).
   - *Parameterization*: Sum intercept constant $C \in [2, 6]$ for lines $y = x$ and $y = C - x$.
   - *Constraint strategy*: The lines meet at $(C/2,C/2)$. Horizontal slices for washers run from $x=y$ to $x=C-y$ throughout $0 \le y \le C/2$, so disks/washers about the $y$-axis require 1 integral. Vertical shells split at $x=C/2$ and require 2 integrals.
   - *Canonical solution*: `1` (exact integer count for the disk/washer method integrating with respect to $y$).
   - *Verification strategy*: `math.verify@1` exact numeric matching.
   - *Expected difficulty*: 1–2 (Conceptual Diagnostic).
   - *Limitations*: Marked `needs-review`: the geometry above is independently verified for the stated region, but the unavailable source example and its provenance note claim the opposite count and require source-text reconciliation.

## Structural inferences
1. **Explicit Separation of Horizontal and Vertical Shells**: While OpenStax groups both under Section 2.3, we separated `shell-method-vertical-axis` and `shell-method-horizontal-axis` into distinct concept nodes. This enables Practice to diagnose variable-of-integration confusion ($dx$ vs. $dy$).
2. **Atomic Diagnostic Objective for Setup**: OpenStax blends setup and evaluation into worked examples. We created `shell.setup-radius-height-y-axis` and `pf-shell-setup-integrand` to isolate integrand assembly from antiderivative computation.
3. **Formalized Parameter Invariants**: OpenStax provides concrete numbers (e.g. $y = 2x - x^2$, $[0, 2]$); dependent integer bounds use `{ "parameter": "<id>", "offset": <integer> }`, with the referenced parameter sampled first and the bound evaluated before sampling the dependent parameter.
4. **Manifest Content Inventory**: `package.json` explicitly lists concept, objective, and problem-family IDs so consumers do not need to directory-scan this package. These arrays describe current content only and do not attempt to define the future Knowledge Package schema.
5. **Single-pass Template Substitution**: Templates replace exact `{key}` tokens once and do not evaluate arithmetic. Nested LaTeX grouping such as `x^{{n}+1}` preserves the exponent expression after substituting `n`.

## Unresolved gaps
1. **Right-shifted Vertical Axes**: OpenStax Section 2.3 mentions in text that vertical lines in the right half-plane can be used (where radius is $k - x$), but primarily details left-shifted axes ($x = -k$). Right-shifted vertical axes are omitted from verified families here to avoid ambiguity until multi-axis templates are verified.
2. **Washer vs. Shell Direct Equality Benchmarks**: Example 2.17 mentions ease of evaluation, but does not provide a formal metric for algebraic complexity beyond the number of integrals.
3. **Example 2.17 Source Reconciliation**: For the stated triangle revolved about the y-axis, direct geometry gives one washer integral in $y$ and two shell integrals in $x$. The package's source text is unavailable, while `provenance.json` records the opposite claim; the affected family remains `needs-review` until the source example can be checked.
4. **External Disk/Washer Prerequisite**: `method-selection-volume-of-revolution` depends on disk/washer knowledge as well as both shell-method concepts, but this package has no disk/washer concept to link. Cross-package prerequisite representation belongs to the future Knowledge Package schema.
